                         MANAGERIAL ECONOMICS
Course Overview
This course introduces M.Tech students to the principles of managerial economics
and their application in technology-driven business environments. It equips
students with analytical tools to understand market structures, firm behaviour,
pricing strategies, demand-supply dynamics, and decision-making under
uncertainty.
The course bridges engineering and business thinking, enabling students to
evaluate the economic consequences of technical decisions, understand the
competitive landscape of technology markets, and develop a foundation for
entrepreneurship, product management, and technology leadership roles.
Course Structure & Syllabus
1. Foundations of Managerial Economics
    ● Nature and scope of managerial economics; its relation to microeconomics
       and decision science
    ● Scarcity, choice, and opportunity cost in technology resource allocation
    ● Marginal analysis and optimisation: the core decision-making framework
    ● Economic profit vs. accounting profit; the role of economic reasoning in
       engineering decisions
    ● Time value of money: present value and future value basics
    ● Case: Resource allocation decisions in a software product team

2. Demand Analysis & Forecasting
   ● Law of demand; demand functions and demand curves for technology
       products
   ● Determinants of demand: price, income, substitutes, complements, and
       network effects
   ● Price elasticity of demand: concept, measurement, and managerial
       applications
   ● Income elasticity and cross-price elasticity
   ● Demand for digital goods: zero marginal cost, bundling, and versioning
   ● Demand forecasting methods: qualitative (Delphi, expert opinion) and
       quantitative (time series, regression)
   ● Case: Estimating demand elasticity
   ● Demand estimation using real datasets (industry/API data)
   ● Machine learning approaches to forecasting (basic intro)

3. Production & Cost Analysis
    ● Production functions: short run vs. long run; total, average, and marginal
        product
    ● Law of diminishing marginal returns and its implications for scaling tech
        teams
    ● Isoquants and isocosts; optimal input combinations
    ● Cost concepts: fixed, variable, total, average, and marginal costs
    ● Economies of scale, economies of scope, and learning curve effects in
        technology industries
    ● Cost structure of software and AI firms: high fixed costs, near-zero marginal
        costs
    ● Break-even analysis and contribution margin
   ● Case: Cost analysis of cloud vs. on-premise infrastructure deployment
   ● Productivity analytics (TFP, efficiency frontier)
   ● Cost optimization using operations research techniques
   ● Capacity planning in cloud/AI systems

4. Market Structures & Competitive Strategy
   ● Overview of market structures: perfect competition, monopolistic competition,
      oligopoly, monopoly
   ● Profit maximisation under different market structures
   ● Monopoly and market power: sources, welfare implications, and regulation
   ● Monopolistic competition and product differentiation in technology markets
   ● Oligopoly models: Cournot, Bertrand, and Stackelberg competition
   ● Digital markets and platform economics: two-sided markets, network effects,
      and winner-take-all dynamics
   ● Antitrust and competition policy in the technology sector
   ● Porter's Five Forces applied to technology industries
   ● Case: Market structure analysis of cloud computing, ride-hailing, and search
      engine markets
5. Pricing Strategies in Technology Markets
    ● Pricing objectives and the role of elasticity in pricing decisions
    ● Cost-plus pricing, value-based pricing, and competitive pricing
    ● Price discrimination: first, second, and third degree; implementation in digital
       platforms
    ● Freemium models, tiered pricing, and subscription economics
    ● Dynamic pricing and algorithmic pricing: applications in e-commerce and
       ride-sharing
    ● Bundling strategies: pure bundling, mixed bundling, and tying
    ● Penetration pricing vs. skimming pricing for new technology products
    ● Transfer pricing and pricing in multi-division technology firms
    ● Case: Pricing strategy design for an AI-as-a-service product

6. Game Theory & Strategic Decision-Making
   ● Introduction to game theory: players, strategies, payoffs, and Nash equilibrium
   ● Prisoner's dilemma and its applications to technology competition (price wars,
       patent races)
   ● Dominant strategies and iterated elimination of dominated strategies
   ● Sequential games and backward induction; first-mover and second-mover
       advantages
   ● Repeated games and cooperation: implications for strategic alliances and
       open-source ecosystems
   ● Mixed strategies and equilibrium
   ● Bayesian games (incomplete information)
   ● Mechanism design basics (auctions, incentives)
   ● Applications in: ad auctions (Google Ads), spectrum auctions, and platform
       competition
7. Macroeconomics & the Business Environment
   ● Key macroeconomic indicators: GDP, inflation, unemployment, and their
      relevance to technology business planning
   ● Business cycles and their effect on technology investment and hiring
   ● Monetary policy and interest rates: impact on technology firm valuations and
      capital costs
   ● Fiscal policy, government spending on R&D, and industrial policy for
      technology sectors
   ● Foreign exchange rates and globalisation of technology supply chains
   ● India's digital economy: key indicators, policy frameworks (Digital India, PLI
      schemes), and growth drivers
   ● Case: Macro-environment analysis for expanding a technology startup
      internationally
