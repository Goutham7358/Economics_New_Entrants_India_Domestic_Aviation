Akasa Air not yet cash-positive but well-capitalised; Jhunjhunwala family reinvests: CEO Vinay Dube
Akasa Air reported topline growth of nearly 50 per cent, while EBITDA margins expanded by around 50 per cent during the period.
We are building a financial foundation for generations to come and we are pleased with the progress so far, according to Dube.
Edited by: Ankur GuptaJoin UsIndia’s airline firm -- Akasa Air -- is prioritising disciplined and conservative expansion over breakneck growth as it scales up operations in a highly competitive aviation market, founder and CEO Vinay Dube said in an exclusive interaction with ET Now. The airline firm's CEO said the firm delivered a strong performance in the second half of calendar year 2025, which also marked the first half of FY26. “Our topline continues to grow, margins are improving and our network keeps expanding. It has been a phenomenal year for us,” he said.Akasa Air reported topline growth of nearly 50 per cent, while EBITDA margins expanded by around 50 per cent during the period. The airline now operates 150 daily departures, serving 26 domestic and six international routes. Its codeshare partnership with Etihad Airways is also progressing well, Dube added.
Focus on planning, conservative expansionEmphasising the importance of planning, the airline CEO said Akasa’s philosophy has been to grow cautiously in an industry prone to volatility. “India is the world’s fastest-growing aviation economy. Planning and being conservative are essential. If you chase the last penny, troubles get transferred to employees, customers or investors,” Dube said.The airline’s approach, he noted, is aimed at building a sustainable business for decades rather than short-term gains. Recent industry-wide disruptions have only reinforced the need for conservative planning, he added.
Well-capitalised to manage volatilityOn financial strength, Dube said Akasa is well-capitalised and recently closed its second round of funding in August. The airline currently operates 31 aircraft and expects aircraft deliveries at a pace of more than one per month, with fleet growth accelerating through 2026. Akasa has a firm aircraft order book of 226 planes.“Being well-capitalised is a hedge against fuel volatility, foreign exchange risk and geopolitical disruptions,” Dube said, adding that the airline would rather own a smaller share of a strong company than a larger share of one operating on the edge.
Jhunjhunwala family, new investors double downDube confirmed that the Jhunjhunwala family -- early backers of Akasa Air -- participated meaningfully in the second funding round. New investors such as Premji Invest, 361 Degrees and Claypond Capital -- the family office of Ranjan Pai -- also joined the shareholder base.“These are high-quality, patient investors with strong governance standards,” he said.YouTube Video Loading...
Profitability and cash flow to follow growthWhile Akasa is not yet generating cash from operations, Dube said the airline is ahead of its financial roadmap. Strong consumer response, rising unit revenues and robust demand from corporate travellers have supported growth.The airline now has over 1,300 corporate contracts, an uncommon feat for an airline of its age and scale, Dube said. “Corporates are asking us to add more aircraft and destinations,” he added.On costs, Akasa is tracking ahead of its internal plans, reinforcing its focus on cost leadership. “We are building a financial foundation for generations to come, and we are pleased with the progress so far,” Dube said.(Interviewed by Sajeet Manghat)(Disclaimer: The above article is meant for informational purposes only, and should not be considered as any investment advice.
