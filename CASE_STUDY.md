# O3: Vistara, TruJet and Akasa Air

**Observation cutoff: 31 July 2026.** This case study compares Vistara, which merged into Air India; TruJet, which ceased flying; and Akasa, which remained operational. Competition is assessed on the airport pairs they served. National traffic describes their overall scale; financial accounts help explain whether expansion was sustainable.

## 1. Economic framework

The [managerial economics syllabus](sources/DA241o_Syllabus.pdf) provides three useful concepts:

- **Economies of scale and scope (Section 3).** In the simple cost model `AC = F/Q + v`, greater output spreads fixed cost over more units. A combined network can also share resources. Fuel costs, aircraft costs and weak demand can offset these benefits.
- **Market definition, oligopoly and differentiation (Sections 4–5).** Use a nonstop airport pair, combining both directions, as the working market. Vistara offered a premium product, Akasa followed a low-cost model, and TruJet focused on regional connections. Their competitive positions depend on which rivals served each pair.
- **Break-even and accounting profit (Sections 1 and 3).** Revenue must cover costs for profit to become positive. Funding can support operations while losses continue. Accounting profit also excludes the opportunity cost of investors' capital, which would be needed to measure economic profit.

These concepts guide the interpretation; the available accounts do not identify an exact minimum efficient scale or prove the causes of closure.

## 2. Competition on the routes served

We have monthly passenger totals for airport pairs and airline identities in approved schedules. Only the schedules supply an airline split. The following shares therefore measure **scheduled frequency**, pooling both directions:

| Airline | Route and month | Airline flights / recorded route flights | Frequency share | Brand HHI |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| TruJet | Chennai–Salem, Nov 2019 | 60 / 60 | 100.00% | 10,000.00 |
| TruJet | Chennai–Kadapa, Nov 2019 | 60 / 60 | 100.00% | 10,000.00 |
| Vistara | Mumbai–Delhi, Nov 2023 | 951 / 3,191 | 29.80% | 2,815.08 |
| Akasa | Mumbai–Delhi, Nov 2023 | 180 / 3,191 | 5.64% | 2,815.08 |
| Akasa | Delhi–Pune, Jun 2026 | 120 / 1,430 | 8.39% | 2,744.88 |

**Calculation:** route share = `100 × airline flights / recorded route flights`; HHI = `sum of squared percentage shares of all recorded airline brands`. TruJet's Chennai–Salem filing shows one daily departure each way: `30 + 30 = 60` in November. The [original DGCA report](sources/route_schedules/TruJet_WS2019.pdf), pp. 2 and 4, confirms both airport records.

TruJet's national passenger share was **0.63%** that month. Its local role was substantial: on **17 of 22 screened routes**, it supplied at least half the recorded scheduled flights. Eight further routes in its filing could not be assessed reliably. Actual city-pair returns record **3,281 passengers on Chennai–Salem** and **3,191 on Chennai–Kadapa**, across all airlines. High local share in a small market can coexist with difficulty covering fixed costs.

On Mumbai–Delhi, Vistara's recorded frequency share increased from **20.01% in April 2019 to 31.27% in February 2024**. Akasa's increased from **2.65% in December 2022 to 8.75% in July 2026**. These are within-route comparisons over available observations; they are not matched entry-age comparisons.

The schedules describe planned supply. Matching airport records cannot rule out an entirely missing competitor filing. Brand HHI also requires ownership grouping before interpreting it as firm concentration; Vistara and Air India already shared Tata ownership in 2023. [Route data, coverage and calculation details](ROUTE_DATA.md).

The national traffic records contain **118, 79 and 48 positive passenger months** for Vistara, TruJet and Akasa, respectively. These include partial launch/exit months and pandemic interruptions. Akasa's eventual operating duration remains unknown.

## 3. Growth and financial performance

The following inputs come from financial tables labelled audited in the ICRA reports, PDF page 4 of each. Figures cover company operations, including international services. Vistara's figures are standalone; Akasa's are consolidated.

| Measure | Vistara, FY2023 → FY2024 | Akasa, FY2025 → FY2026 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Operating income, ₹ crore | 12,261.4 → 15,310.3 | 4,582.7 → 6,167.9 |
| Profit after tax, ₹ crore | −1,393.3 → −581.1 | −1,983.5 → −2,716.0 |
| Revenue growth | +24.87% | +34.59% |
| Change in net loss magnitude | −58.29% | +36.93% |
| Net profit margin | −11.36% → −3.80% | −43.28% → −44.03% |
| Reported operating margin | 4.0% → 14.6% | −23.0% → −25.6% |

Revenue growth = `100 × (later income / earlier income − 1)`; net margin = `100 × profit after tax / operating income`. Thus Vistara's FY2024 margin is `100 × −581.1 / 15,310.3 = −3.80%`. Net margins improved by **7.57 percentage points** for Vistara and deteriorated by **0.75 points** for Akasa. Reported operating margin here means profit before depreciation, interest, tax and amortisation divided by operating income.

## 4. Decisions, funding and outcomes

**TruJet: regional expansion followed by a financing crisis.** Its substantial local presence did not ensure enough cash to sustain operations. BusinessLine reported in October 2021 that an agreed Interups investment had not arrived and MEIL had transferred ownership back to Umesh. The managing director attributed MEIL's exit to pandemic cash outflows and aviation being outside its core business. By February 2022, contemporary reports describe financing delays, aircraft groundings and operations reduced to one ATR. Management sought **US$25 million** and replacement management, but this was a funding proposal, with receipt unverified. The evidence supports cash shortages and aircraft constraints behind cessation; it cannot identify which routes lost money or assign the entire failure to one decision. [Ownership and funding report](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/ex-promoter-vankayalapati-umesh-regains-control-of-trujet-from-meil/article36971346.ece); [cessation report and management response](https://www.ch-aviation.com/news/112730-indias-trujet-grounds-fleet-in-talks-for-25mn-funding).

CAG subsequently recorded **42 commencements from 52 UDAN awards**, or **80.77%**, and zero operational in March 2023. These are UDAN phases 1–3 awards, with a different route definition from our pooled airport pairs. [CAG](https://d7i5wg8xwe4hf.cloudfront.net/uploads/download_audit_report/2023/Report-No.22-English-064d5dc4be9bf67.48739275.pdf), PDF pp. 38, 103.

**Akasa: raising capital while building scale.** Akasa confirmed completion of an investment round on **19 August 2025**, involving Premji Invest, funds managed by 360 ONE Asset, Claypond Capital and further Jhunjhunwala-family capital. Business Standard estimated **about US$125 million**; the company announcement does not disclose the amount. ICRA also identifies aircraft sale-and-leaseback inflows as funding its cash losses: aircraft are sold to a lessor and leased back, releasing cash while creating future payment obligations. These sources explain how expansion can continue during losses. [Company announcement](https://www.akasaair.com/news-room/akasa-press-releases/akasa-air-announces-completion-of-strategic-investment-transaction-welcomes-new-investment-partners-to-the-akasian-family); [funding report, PDF p. 2](sources/akasa_funding_business_standard_2025_08_20.pdf); [ICRA](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRationalReportFilePdf?id=143563), pp. 2–3.

Management's stated route to profitability combines lower unit costs, fuller aircraft, ancillary and cargo income, corporate customers and network expansion. This fits spreading fixed costs and diversifying revenue. Its [July 2025 profitability release](https://www.akasaair.com/news-room/akasa-press-releases/akasa-air-reports-strong-financial-performance-in-fy-24-25-remains-on-path-to-profitability) gives no firm net-profit date. ICRA still expected medium-term cash losses in June 2026. Continued operation at our cutoff is therefore an interim outcome.

**Vistara: its owners chose integration with Air India.** Tata and Singapore Airlines had infused **₹10,020 crore** into Vistara by ICRA's August 2024 report. Premium service attracted customers, but domestic fare competition constrained pricing and net losses continued. After Tata acquired Air India, the owners chose to combine Air India's slots and traffic rights with Vistara's customers and operating capabilities. Their stated aim was better network and resource use, consistent with economies of scale and scope. The merger completed on **12 November 2024**; SIA exchanged its Vistara stake and paid **₹2,058.5 crore to Air India** for 25.1% of the enlarged airline. The evidence documents a shareholder strategy; it does not establish that Vistara had to close or quantify realised merger savings. [ICRA](https://www.icra.in/Rating/GetRationalReportFilePdf?id=129364), pp. 1–2; [merger rationale](https://links.sgx.com/FileOpen/NE-0922.ashx?App=Announcement&FileID=740172), pp. 2–3; [completion filing](https://links.sgx.com/1.0.0/corporate-announcements/WN9KAJ4AZRKNOFUJ/845637_Rule%20706A_May%202025.pdf), p. 1.

The [short supporting note](ACTIONS_AND_FUNDING.md) gives the funding status, management's profitability measures and the limits of these explanations.

## 5. Two hypotheses and the results

**H1: TruJet supplied at least half the scheduled flights on a majority of its covered routes in November 2019.** Of 22 screened routes, **17 pass: 77.27%**. The recovered schedules support this hypothesis for that sample. Local significance is therefore a better description of TruJet's role than its national size alone.

**H2: Revenue growth was accompanied by an improvement in net profit margin.** Compare revenue and margin changes for the two available financial pairs. Vistara supports this prediction; Akasa does not. Revenue growth alone did not ensure improving margins in these observations.

These are descriptive checks on selected routes and financial periods. Missing schedules and different observation windows limit wider conclusions; neither check establishes causation.

## 6. A simple break-even exercise

For a classroom sensitivity calculation, assume a fraction `c` of extra revenue contributes to profit, with tax and other profit items unchanged. Then:

`Extra revenue needed to close the accounting loss = existing loss / c`.

| Latest financial year | If c = 100% | If c = 50% |
|---|---:|---:|
| Vistara FY2024 | ₹581.1 crore; 3.80% of base revenue | ₹1,162.2 crore; 7.59% |
| Akasa FY2026 | ₹2,716.0 crore; 44.03% of base revenue | ₹5,432.0 crore; 88.07% |

Both contribution rates are assumptions. The exercise shows how additional costs raise the revenue required to cover a loss. Exact break-even passenger volumes need fixed costs, variable costs and passenger revenue on a consistent basis. Net losses also cannot substitute for cash outflows in a cash-runway calculation.
