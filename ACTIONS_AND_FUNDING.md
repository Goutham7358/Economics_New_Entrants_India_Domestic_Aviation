# O3: why the airlines had different outcomes

**Cutoff: 31 July 2026.** Read with the [case study](CASE_STUDY.md). The evidence supports the following sequence of decisions and constraints; it does not estimate the causal effect of each decision.

| Airline | Decisions and constraints | Economic interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| TruJet | Built a regional network; sought replacement investors as cash support weakened; ownership changed and promised funding remained outstanding; aircraft availability contracted before flights stopped in February 2022. | A large share of a small route can coexist with insufficient cash to meet aircraft and operating commitments. |
| Akasa | Raised additional equity, used aircraft sale-and-leaseback transactions and expanded its network while working on unit costs and revenue. | Access to capital gives time to spread fixed costs and develop demand. It also creates dependence on continued funding and aircraft deliveries. |
| Vistara | Built a premium airline with parent support; shareholders then combined it with Air India following Tata's acquisition of that airline. | Differentiation helped attract customers; integration offered expected economies of scale and scope. |

## TruJet: what connects the funding problem to cessation?

BusinessLine's **12 October 2021** report says Interups had not supplied the agreed investment and MEIL transferred ownership to Umesh. It reproduces his explanation that pandemic cash outflows and MEIL's business priorities prompted the handover. This documents an interruption in financial backing. The February 2022 report then describes the shrinking available fleet and the proposed rescue. The economic mechanism is that inadequate cash can restrict maintenance and leasing, reducing flights and the ability to earn revenue. This is an interpretation of the sequence, rather than an audited allocation of the causes of failure. [October report and management account](sources/trujet_funding_ownership_2021_10_12.html).

The legal records in the source pack should not be used to label the outcome liquidation: the 2021 insolvency admission was subsequently withdrawn, and the 2024 lease petition was dismissed over a disputed claim.

## Akasa: management's path to profitability

Akasa's own **22 July 2025** release explains four ways it aims to improve the business:

1. **Lower cost per seat-kilometre:** efficient operations, technology and expansion that spreads overheads.
2. **Earn more from capacity:** stronger distribution, corporate customers and network development.
3. **Add revenue streams:** ancillary products and cargo alongside passenger fares.
4. **Maintain financial support:** fresh capital to support expansion while the business develops.

The following are management's reported changes, not calculations reconstructed from full accounts:

| Company measure | FY2025 release | FY2026 release |
|---|---:|---:|
| Stage-adjusted revenue per available seat-kilometre (RASK) | +13% | +10% |
| Cost per available seat-kilometre (CASK) | −7%, **excluding fuel** | −4%, fuel exclusion not stated |

[FY2025 release](sources/akasa_profitability_plan_2025.html); [FY2026 release](sources/akasa_management_results_2026_06_23.html). The cost definitions differ, so the two reductions are not a consistent series. Absolute RASK and CASK levels are missing; these percentage changes do not establish break-even. Reported EBITDAR improvement also excludes costs included in net profit, including aircraft rentals, depreciation and interest.

In a [January 2026 interview](sources/akasa_ceo_interview_2026_01_07.html), CEO Vinay Dube described conservative expansion and investor capital as protection against volatility. The releases reviewed give **no firm date for net profitability**. ICRA's June 2026 report still expects medium-term cash losses, with delivery delays threatening sale-and-leaseback inflows. Our calculations therefore retain the audited financial table reported by ICRA; management's unit measures describe its strategy separately. [ICRA, pp. 2–4](sources/akasa_icra_2026_06_12.pdf).

## Vistara: why merger was attractive to its owners

SIA's November 2022 announcement identifies Air India's slots and traffic rights and Vistara's service capabilities and customers as complementary assets. Combining them was expected to improve network and resource use and give SIA participation in a business four to five times Vistara's scale. These are stated strategic reasons; reduced losses before completion do not show that the merger was unnecessary or that its promised savings were achieved. [Announcement, pp. 2–3](sources/vistara_merger_rationale_2022_11_29.pdf).
